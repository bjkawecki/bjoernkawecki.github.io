For many people, documenting sounds like grey forms and a tiresome duty. I want to invite a shift in perspective.

Let us treat documenting as something magical: something that allows us time travel.

What actually happens when we document? People turn an experience into information, give a moment a form, and hold on to something that had only just been unique to that moment. A note. A photo. A set of instructions. A book. A map. Suddenly something bound to a particular person and a particular instant can leave that instant and become reachable for others.

This happens in the large and in the small. Sacred texts outlast their authors. Histories let past events resurface. A researcher writes in a journal. Whether a few lines or whole books, the gesture is the same. Something is meant to remain for others. Knowledge moves from one mind to the next, from one moment to the next, across years or millennia.

Long before anyone called it “documentation”, people were making such time travel possible. Documentation connects two points in time that would otherwise have nothing to do with each other. That is why we can read documentation as an invitation to someone we do not know, or who does not even exist yet. To that someone we can say: this is what I lived, this is what I experienced, this is what I learned, and if you want, you can continue from here.
